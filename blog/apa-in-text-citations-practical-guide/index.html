APA In-Text Citations: A Practical Guide

In-text citations connect your claims to your sources. Here is how to format them in APA style, with examples you can adapt.

By the Rosjos team November 17, 2026 6 min read

In-text citations tell readers where your ideas come from. In APA style, each citation points to a full entry in your reference list. The format is simple once you learn a few patterns, and most mistakes come from small details like punctuation or ampersands.

The basic pattern

A parenthetical citation includes the author's last name and the year of publication, separated by a comma: (Rivera, 2021). You can also name the author in the sentence itself and put the year in parentheses: Rivera (2021) found that practice improved recall. Choose whichever form reads more naturally, but keep the information the same.

One, two, or three or more authors

With two authors, include both names every time. Inside parentheses, join them with an ampersand: (Rivera & Chen, 2021). In the sentence itself, use the word “and”: Rivera and Chen (2021) reported similar results.

With three or more authors, use the first author's last name followed by “et al.” starting with your first citation: (Patel et al., 2020).

Quotations need page numbers

When you quote directly, include the page number after the year: (Rivera, 2021, p. 14). Keep quotations short, and use them only when the exact wording matters. Most of the time, paraphrasing your source in your own words, with a citation, is clearer and shows you understand the idea.

Works without an author

If a source has no author, cite its title in place of the author's name, using a shortened version of the title if it is long. Check your assignment's style guide or your instructor for guidance on how titles should be formatted in the text.

Make sure your reference list matches

Every in-text citation should match an entry in your reference list, and every entry should be cited somewhere in the paper. Before you submit, check the spelling of each name and the year. A quick cross-check catches most errors.

When in doubt, check the source

The official APA Style website and the APA publication manual are the best references for specific cases. Your school's writing center or librarians can also help with unusual sources.
