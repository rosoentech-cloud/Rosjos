How to Study for a Test in One Week

Limited time doesn't have to mean panic. A focused seven-day plan puts your effort where it will help most.

By the Rosjos team November 17, 2026 6 min read

When a test is a week away, it's tempting to reread everything and hope it sticks. A better approach is to find out what you don't know yet, then spend your limited time closing those gaps through practice.

Start with the test itself

Find out the format, the topics covered, how many questions there are, and how much time you'll have. Gather past quizzes, practice questions, and any review sheets your teacher or instructor provided. Knowing the format tells you what kind of practice to do.

A day-by-day plan

  1. Day 1: Take a short practice test under timed conditions. Mark every miss and note why it happened: a concept gap, a careless error, or running out of time.
  2. Days 2 and 3: Work on your weakest topics. For each one, review a short explanation, work two examples, then close your notes and solve two problems from memory.
  3. Day 4: Redo every question you missed on Day 1 without looking at your old answers. Anything you still get wrong goes on a short list for the next day.
  4. Day 5: Take a second timed practice test. Compare your score and your pacing with Day 1.
  5. Day 6: Focus on what is still weak, and review your list of recurring mistakes.
  6. Day 7: Do a light review only. Skim your summary sheet, answer a few questions you already know well for confidence, and go to bed early.

Protect your sleep

Sleep plays a well-documented role in how the brain consolidates memories. Staying up late to cram usually trades a small amount of extra study time for worse performance the next day. If you have to choose between one more hour of studying and a full night's sleep, choose sleep.

Practice beats rereading

Whenever you can, test yourself instead of rereading. Close the book, write what you remember, and then check. The article on active recall in this blog explains why this works so well. You'll find the gaps faster, and the effort itself helps you remember more.

A week is enough time to make real progress if you spend it on the right things. Start with a test, fix what it shows you, and repeat.
