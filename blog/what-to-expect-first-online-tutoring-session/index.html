What to Expect in Your First Online Tutoring Session

A first session can feel uncertain. Here is what usually happens, what to bring, and how to make the most of your time.

By the Rosjos team November 17, 2026 5 min read

A first tutoring session can feel awkward. You may not know what to bring, what the tutor will ask, or whether you're expected to understand the material already. Knowing the usual flow takes away much of the uncertainty, so you can focus on learning.

Prepare before you log on

Gather your class notes, the assignment or practice set you're stuck on, and any feedback you've received on earlier work. Then identify the exact point where you get lost. Saying “I don't understand this chapter” is a fine starting point, but “I follow the first step but freeze when I have to combine two formulas” gives your tutor something specific to work with.

Check your internet connection, find a quiet space, and keep a pen and paper nearby. Writing down steps as you go helps you remember how you solved a problem, not just that it was solved.

What usually happens

Most first sessions start with a short conversation about your goals, your upcoming deadlines, and what has been hardest so far. A good tutor asks questions to find out what you already know, rather than assuming you know nothing or everything.

After that, the session usually moves to the material itself. A common pattern is simple: the tutor explains one idea, works through one example, and then asks you to try a similar problem while they watch. Expect to explain your thinking out loud. A wrong answer is useful information, because it shows exactly where the confusion is.

Questions worth asking

  • What is the most important skill in this topic?
  • Which mistakes do students usually make here?
  • How can I check my own work before I submit it?
  • What should I practice before our next session?

After the session

Take two minutes to write down the main thing you learned and one question you still have. Then try one more problem, or reread one section, on your own before the next session. Tutoring works best as a steady routine, so agree on a schedule before you finish.

If something didn't work, say so. Whether the pace, the explanations, or the format feels off, a good tutor wants to know and can adjust the next session.
